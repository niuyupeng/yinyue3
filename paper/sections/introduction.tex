\section{Introduction}

Automatic harmonization is a useful setting for studying accountable music generation. In four-part chorale writing, a pitch that is likely under a statistical model may still be unacceptable when it creates a voice crossing, excessive spacing, unresolved tendency tone, weak cadence, or parallel perfect interval. These errors are visible in the score and can be described in the language of common-practice harmony and counterpoint, which makes the task well suited to neural-symbolic modelling.

Recent neural systems have shown that symbolic models can learn strong distributions over chorale textures. However, prediction likelihood alone does not explain why a generated score violates a voice-leading rule, nor does it provide a direct path for generating MusicXML material that can be inspected by performers, teachers, or analysts. A practical chorale harmonization system therefore needs three properties at once: it must predict missing voices, expose the musical constraints being checked, and report failures at the level of notation rather than only at the level of tokens.

This paper addresses score-level SATB chorale harmonization. The inputs are soprano melodies, bass lines, or partially masked SATB scores, optionally accompanied by key, metrical position, cadence evidence, or an automatically derived harmonic plan. The outputs are complete four-part MusicXML scores and rule-violation reports. The work deliberately excludes audio synthesis and popular-music accompaniment generation. MIDI pitch numbers appear only as internal symbolic tokens.

We implement a complete local research pipeline around this task. The code parses the Bach chorales bundled with music21, builds deterministic train, validation, and test splits, trains neural baselines and a neural-symbolic Transformer, exports generated scores as MusicXML, evaluates common-practice rule diagnostics, generates tables and figures, and prepares materials for blinded expert review. The present manuscript reports only values found in logged result files. Full RTX 4060 Ti primary runs are available for the LSTM baseline, vanilla/no-constraints Transformer, proposed rule-guided Transformer, masked infilling task, soprano-to-SATB task, and three focused ablations. Expert ratings are not yet available and are treated as pending evidence.
