\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Score Representation}

Scores are represented on a fixed symbolic time grid with default quarter length 0.25. Each timestep stores four pitch tokens ordered as soprano, alto, tenor, and bass. Special tokens encode padding, rests, held notes, and masked positions. The tokenizer also records voice identity, beat position, measure index, and known/masked flags so that soprano-conditioned generation and masked SATB infilling can share one representation.

\subsection{Neural Models}

The baseline neural systems are a bidirectional LSTM and a vanilla Transformer encoder over quantized SATB timesteps. Both baselines condition on visible voice tokens, known/masked flags, beat features, measure features, and learned positional embeddings, then predict missing pitch tokens with cross entropy.

\subsection{Proposed Neural-Symbolic Transformer}

The proposed model extends the vanilla Transformer in three explicit ways. First, it replaces purely absolute timestep encoding with learned bidirectional relative-position attention, so that contrapuntal relations can be modeled by local and medium-range temporal distance rather than by absolute index only. Second, it injects automatic harmonic context into the timestep representation, including estimated key, chord root, seventh-chord evidence, dominant-function evidence, phrase-ending flags, and chord/Roman-numeral coverage indicators. Failed analyses are represented as unknown or unavailable labels and are not fabricated. Third, generation uses iterative masked refinement inspired by infilling models: initially masked alto, tenor, and bass positions are predicted, then a deterministic subset of target positions is re-masked and rewritten while conditioning on the current four-part texture. A symbolic rule-guided decoding layer then performs conservative score-level repair and records remaining violations for explanation.

The corresponding ablations remove harmonic conditioning, iterative refinement, or rule-guided decoding while preserving the same SATB data interface. These ablations are intended to separate the contribution of learned harmonic conditioning, non-autoregressive refinement, and explicit common-practice constraints. The implementation does not use audio generation, waveform modeling, drum or bass accompaniment generation, or style-transfer machinery outside the score-level SATB task.

\subsection{Rule-Guided Decoding and Explanation}

The symbolic rule layer is implemented as a transparent analysis and decoding component. It reports voice-range violations, voice crossing, adjacent-voice spacing violations, parallel fifths, parallel octaves, hidden direct perfect intervals, leading-tone behavior, melodic leap recovery, seventh-resolution evidence, and cadence evidence. These checks produce numeric counts, penalties, and human-readable messages such as measure and beat-localized rule explanations. Rule-guided decoding is treated as a lightweight postprocessor and evaluator, not as a fabricated differentiable training loss.

\subsection{Automatic Harmonic-Label Extraction}

For each chorale, music21 estimates a global key. The SATB texture is analyzed on the model's quantized grid to derive vertical sonorities, chord roots, chord qualities, approximate Roman numerals, seventh-chord flags, dominant-function flags, and phrase-end flags when possible. Ambiguous or failed analyses are stored as \texttt{UNKNOWN}. Consequently, Roman numeral coverage and cadence evidence are reported as diagnostic metadata rather than ground-truth musicological annotation.

\subsection{Seventh-Resolution and Cadence Heuristics}

The seventh-resolution checker first looks for confident seventh-like sonorities from automatic labels or pitch-class structure. For a detected chordal seventh, the corresponding voice is expected to resolve downward by semitone or whole tone at the next harmonic event. The cadence checker conservatively classifies phrase-ending or final progressions as authentic-like, half-cadence-like, deceptive-like, or \texttt{UNKNOWN}. Cadence inconsistency is counted only when harmonic evidence is clear.
