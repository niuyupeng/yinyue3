\section*{Abstract}

Four-part chorale harmonization is a compact test bed for symbolic music generation because local pitch accuracy is not sufficient for stylistic validity. A generated SATB score must also respect voice range, spacing, contrapuntal independence, resolution tendencies, and cadential function. We present an explainable neural-symbolic framework for score-level SATB harmonization that accepts a soprano melody, bass line, or partially masked four-part score and returns MusicXML notation with a measure-localized rule report. The method combines a lightweight Transformer with harmonic conditioning, relative temporal attention, iterative masked refinement, and a deterministic rule-guided decoding layer. On the logged RTX 4060 Ti experiments using 371 music21 Bach chorales, the proposed rule-guided Transformer improved soprano-to-SATB pitch accuracy over the vanilla Transformer from 0.7682 to 0.8242 and reduced cross entropy from 0.8708 to 0.5864. It also reduced parallel fifths, voice crossing, spacing violations, and seventh-resolution violations, although total rule violations and parallel octaves were not uniformly reduced. Ablations indicate that harmonic conditioning accounts for much of the likelihood gain, whereas rule-guided decoding mainly changes selected structural diagnostics. The current evidence therefore supports targeted neural-symbolic correction and explainable score analysis, not a complete symbolic constraint solver. Expert evaluation of cadence quality, singability, stylistic consistency, and pedagogical usefulness remains pending.
