\section*{Abstract}

Four-part chorale harmonization is a compact test bed for symbolic music generation because local pitch accuracy is not sufficient for stylistic validity. A generated SATB score must also respect voice range, spacing, contrapuntal independence, resolution tendencies, and cadential function. We present an explainable neural-symbolic framework for score-level SATB harmonization that accepts a soprano melody, bass line, or partially masked four-part score and returns MusicXML notation with a measure-localized rule report. The method combines a lightweight Transformer with harmonic conditioning, relative temporal attention, iterative masked refinement, and a deterministic rule-guided decoding layer. On the logged RTX 4060 Ti experiments using 371 music21 Bach chorales, the proposed rule-guided Transformer improved soprano-to-SATB pitch accuracy over the vanilla Transformer from 0.7682 to 0.8233 and reduced cross entropy from 0.8707 to 0.5942. In the same main comparison, aggregate automatic rule flags decreased from 14.9051 to 3.7823 per 100 score positions, and parallel-octave flags decreased from 2.4117 to 0.0791 per 100 score positions. Logged ablations indicate that harmonic conditioning accounts for much of the likelihood gain, whereas rule-guided decoding mainly changes selected structural diagnostics. The current evidence therefore supports targeted neural-symbolic correction and explainable score analysis, not a complete symbolic constraint solver. Expert evaluation of cadence quality, singability, stylistic consistency, and pedagogical usefulness remains pending.
